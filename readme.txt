## Correct calculation definitions

### 1. VIX zones

Do not hard-code 12, 15, 18 or 22.

A defensible approach is to calculate rolling empirical zones:

```text
Low VIX      = lower historical percentile
Normal VIX   = central historical distribution
High VIX     = upper historical percentile
Extreme VIX  = tail percentile
```

The percentile thresholds must be estimated using the training period and then frozen. They should be calculated separately for:

- Pre-Tuesday regime
- Tuesday regime
- Expiry day
- Non-expiry day
- Event days
- Non-event days

India VIX is based on NIFTY option quotes and a constant approximately 30-day expected-volatility horizon, so it should not be compared directly with HV10 or intraday realized volatility without matching the horizon. [2](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/sites/default/files/inline-files/India_VIX_comp_meth.pdf)

---

### 2. Tuesday OI-wall effectiveness

“Wall effectiveness” is not measurable until “respect” is defined.

One possible pre-registered definition:

```text
Wall respected =
price does not close beyond the wall by more than X points
for Y consecutive minutes before expiry.
```

Then calculate:

```text
Wall effectiveness =
number of respected eligible walls
÷
number of eligible walls
```

Break it down by:

- Wall strength percentile
- Call wall versus put wall
- Distance from spot
- Tuesday versus other days
- Days to expiry
- India VIX percentile
- Intraday time window

Do not define walls using final expiry OI when testing an earlier signal. That would introduce look-ahead bias.

---

### 3. Max-pain accuracy within 50/100 points

Max pain must be calculated using OI available at the actual timestamp.

For each Tuesday:

```text
Error = absolute(expiry settlement price − max pain at timestamp)
```

Then:

```text
Accuracy within 50 points =
count(Error ≤ 50) ÷ total observations
```

and:

```text
Accuracy within 100 points =
count(Error ≤ 100) ÷ total observations
```

You should calculate max pain at multiple snapshots, for example:

```text
10:00
12:00
13:00
14:00
15:00
```

Do not use expiry-day closing OI to test whether max pain predicted the market earlier in the day.

---

### 4. Day-of-week realized volatility

Use synchronized NIFTY spot or futures data and calculate:

```text
Intraday realized variance =
sum of squared intraday log returns
```

Then annualize consistently.

Report separately for:

- Monday
- Tuesday
- Wednesday
- Thursday
- Friday
- Expiry Tuesdays
- Non-expiry Tuesdays
- Pre-September-2025 Thursday regime
- Post-September-2025 Tuesday regime

The pre-change Thursday observations should not simply be pooled with Tuesday observations.

---

### 5. Regime persistence and transition probabilities

Transition probabilities depend entirely on the label definition. There is no objectively correct matrix until regimes are defined.

For labelled regimes \(i\) and \(j\):

```text
P(i → j) =
number of observations transitioning from i to j
÷
number of observations starting in i
```

The matrix must be estimated with:

- Chronological train/validation/test periods
- No random shuffling
- Confidence intervals
- Separate pre- and post-Tuesday regimes
- A minimum-observation rule for each state

For example, a matrix based on arbitrary ADX levels of 18 or 20 would only show persistence of those arbitrary labels. It would not prove that the market naturally has those regimes.

---

### 6. Friday weekend risk premium

“Weekend risk premium” can mean several different things:

1. Friday option IV premium relative to Monday realized volatility.
2. Friday close IV versus Monday open IV.
3. Monday opening gap risk.
4. Friday straddle premium attributable to two non-trading calendar days.
5. Friday-to-Monday option repricing after controlling for news.

These are different measurements.

A defensible study should match:

- Same expiry type
- Same days-to-expiry
- Same moneyness
- Same time of day
- Friday close versus Monday open
- Event days versus ordinary weekends

No single exact percentage should be reported until the definition is fixed.

---

### 7. Exact Tuesday pinning start time

There is no universal pinning start time such as 1:00 PM or 2:00 PM.

You first need a formal rule, for example:

```text
Pinning begins at the first timestamp after which:
absolute(spot − reference price) ≤ X points
and price remains within that band for Y minutes.
```

The reference price must also be specified:

- Max pain
- Highest call OI strike
- Highest put OI strike
- Gamma-weighted level
- Dealer gamma-neutral level

Then estimate the first qualifying timestamp across all Tuesdays. The result should be a distribution, not a single assumed time.

---

### 8. IVR distribution under Tuesday expiry

IV Rank must be calculated using comparable IV observations:

```text
IVR =
(current IV − rolling minimum IV)
÷
(rolling maximum IV − rolling minimum IV)
```

The comparison must use the same:

- Expiry tenor
- Moneyness or delta
- Time of day
- Contract type
- Tuesday/non-Tuesday classification

Using India VIX as a substitute for option IVR would be incorrect because India VIX is a 30-day index-level volatility measure, not the IV of the specific option being traded.

---

### 9. Straddle expected move by day of week

For each timestamp:

```text
ATM straddle =
mid-price of ATM call
+
mid-price of ATM put
```

Then compare it with a precisely defined realized move, such as:

```text
Absolute move from entry timestamp to expiry settlement
```

or:

```text
Absolute move from market open to market close
```

Report:

```text
Realized move / straddle premium
```

by:

- Day of week
- Days to expiry
- VIX zone
- Expiry type
- Opening versus midday entry
- Tuesday expiry versus non-Tuesday expiry

The raw straddle premium is not automatically a one-standard-deviation move. That conversion must be defined and tested.
